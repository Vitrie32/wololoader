<B>The Monklanians (1050 - 1450)<B>

Originating in secluded mountain sanctuaries during the turbulent high Middle Ages, the Monklanians emerged from an ascetic fraternity of scholar-monks who mastered the art of spiritual persuasion and communal fortification. While neighboring realms poured their treasuries into feudal cavalry and mercenary retinues, the Monklanian abbeys focused on spiritual discipline, theological scholarship, and extensive architectural marvels.

By the early twelfth century, the Monklanian abbeys had established an autonomous federation governed by the Council of Abbots. Their emissaries, known widely as Wololo Monks, travelled across Eurasia. Renowned for their vocal resonance and unmatched moral fortitude, these holy men were capable of resolving disputes and turning hostile armies toward their cause without drawing a single blade. Contemporary chronicles frequently marveled at their swift pace and resilience under harsh conditions.

During the fourteenth century, pressure from surrounding empires forced the federation to develop specialized defensive strategies. Replacing traditional heavy knights with holy abbeys and dedicated monastic guards, the Monklanians withstood prolonged sieges through spiritual fervor and steadfast faith. Their legendary chant echoed across valleys, inspiring allies and striking awe into the hearts of their adversaries.

Though secular expansion in the late fifteenth century gradually incorporated the independent monastic domains into larger kingdoms, the cultural and scholarly legacy of the Monklanians endured in monasteries, illuminated manuscripts, and the enduring phrase "Wololo."
